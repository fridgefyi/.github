# fridgefyi

**Food is a cycle. Waste is where it breaks.**

Everything in your fridge arrived at the end of a long chain — soil, water,
sunlight, a season of growing, hands that picked it, miles that carried it.
When it goes in the bin, the food is the smallest part of what's lost. The
water, the land, the fuel, the labour: all of it was already spent, and none
of it comes back.

We build software that helps people cook from what they already have, so that
more of what enters a kitchen is actually eaten.

## What we're building

- **Know what's on hand.** A pantry that reflects the real one: what you have,
  how much, and how long it has left.
- **Know the food.** A large, normalised corpus of real recipes, cleaned so
  that "1 c. firmly packed brown sugar" and "one cup brown sugar" are
  understood to be the same thing.
- **Plan across meals, not one at a time.** This is the hard part, and it is
  where most of our research goes. Finding a single recipe is easy. Choosing a
  *set* of meals that between them finish what you have — in whole units,
  before anything spoils — is a genuinely difficult problem, and solving it
  well is the difference between a suggestion and a plan.

## What we care about

- **Use what exists.** The most sustainable ingredient is the one already in
  your fridge. Every feature is judged by whether it gets that ingredient
  eaten.
- **Numbers you can trust.** Quantities are handled exactly. A plan that says
  you will use the whole bunch means the whole bunch.
- **Reproducible by construction.** Pinned, hermetic builds. What we run is
  what you would get.
- **Your fridge is your business.** What you eat is intimate data. We collect
  what the system needs to work, and not the rest.
- **Enjoyment is the point.** This is not an app about eating your leftovers
  as penance. Cooking well from what you have is the older skill, and the more
  pleasurable one. Less waste is what happens when it goes right.

## Status

Early, and building. Most repositories are private while the core comes
together — this page is where the public story lives, and it will grow as
pieces open up.
